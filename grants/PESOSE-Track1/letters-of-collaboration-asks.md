# Letters of Collaboration — Ready to Send (September PESOSE)

*The gating item. Send all asks the week of June 16 with an **August 7** due date.*

PESOSE Track 1 requires **at least 3 and at most 5** letters of collaboration from **external** users or contributors who are **not** on the proposing team. Because Fall 2026 pilots will not have run by September 1, these are **forward-looking commitment letters**: the writer states who they are, why EduCloud matters to them (typically the GitHub Classroom migration), and that they commit to adopt, test, or contribute in Fall 2026 and beyond. For a *planning*-grant Track 1, that posture is appropriate — but confirm the framing with John before sending, since he knows how a panel reads these.

Two hard constraints: each letter is **≤2 pages**, and the writer must be outside the team (so anyone who becomes a co-PI or senior personnel — possibly John — drops off this list). UArizona is the submitting institution, so **UArizona faculty are not "external"** and are excluded from this pool.

## Template A — user / adopter letter (forward-looking)

*The writer puts this on letterhead, edits the bracketed parts, signs. Keep it to one page. NSF prefers substance over flattery — concrete need, concrete commitment.*

> [Date]
>
> Dear Members of the Review Panel,
>
> I am [name], [title] at [institution], where I teach [courses / programs] serving approximately [N] students per year. I am writing to commit my [course / program / department] as an adopter and testing partner for EduCloud should this project be funded, and to describe why it matters to us.
>
> With GitHub Classroom decommissioned in August 2026, my [courses] face a forced migration. The replacements available to us are commercial, hosted, per-seat services that place student data in third-party systems — a poor fit for [our privacy obligations / our budget constraints / our institutional context]. EduCloud's self-hostable, host-agnostic, privacy-by-design approach directly addresses this gap, and no other option I am aware of does so while remaining free to self-host.
>
> Concretely, I commit to: pilot EduCloud in [course(s)] in [Fall 2026 / academic year 2026–27] with approximately [N] students; provide structured feedback on deployment, grading workflows, and migration from GitHub Classroom; and participate in the project's user-community activities (interviews, workshops). [If applicable: I also expect to contribute (assignments, documentation, bug reports) to the shared project.]
>
> I have no role in the proposing team and offer this letter as an independent prospective user. I am glad to support this effort and believe a governed, sustainable, open-source successor to GitHub Classroom is genuinely needed.
>
> Sincerely,
> [name, title, institution, email]
