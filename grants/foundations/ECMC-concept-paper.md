**Working title:** Infrastructure for Transfer Momentum: open, privacy-preserving computing coursework that travels with the student

**Request:** $550,000 over 30 months

## The problem ECMC cares about, in our corner of it

Community-college students who transfer into computing and data-science majors lose momentum at a specific, fixable seam: the tools change. At the two-year college, coursework lives in ad-hoc folders, LMS uploads, or a borrowed corporate platform; at the four-year institution, it lives in professional version-control workflows the continuing students have used since their first term. The transfer student arrives mid-pipeline as a beginner in the *workflow* even when they are not a beginner in the *content* — one more signal, on top of all the others, that they don't belong. The August 2026 retirement of GitHub Classroom makes this worse: the two-year institutions least equipped to evaluate and migrate to replacement tooling are most likely to fall back to nothing.

## The intervention

EduCloud is an open-source, self-hostable assignment platform (distribution, collection, sandboxed autograding, immediate feedback) built so the *same* workflow runs at both ends of a transfer pathway — and so the student's work, in their own Git account, travels with them across the boundary. Privacy is architectural: the platform stores Git usernames, never student records, which removes the FERPA/data-sharing negotiation that usually kills cross-institutional tooling. It is free to self-host forever; a managed hosted tier (philanthropy-subsidized onboarding, on the model CUNY's Transfer Explorer validated in 2026) serves colleges with no IT capacity.

In Fall 2026 we piloted the platform across a two-year → four-year pathway ([Pima Community College → University of Arizona, CIS 129/185 → ISTA 130/CSC 110, via the NSF-funded STEM Bridge partnership] / [Chemeketa CC → Willamette]) and at four-year adopters across institution types. **[PILOT DATA: n students, n courses, completion/feedback-turnaround/persistence indicators, instructor and student quotes.]**

## What ECMC funding does

Scale the pathway model from one validated pair to **six two-year → four-year pathway pairs** (prioritizing HSIs and rural-serving colleges), with: subsidized hosted onboarding and instructor support for the two-year side; a mastery-based, offline-capable data-literacy/Python bridge curriculum (openly licensed) co-designed with two-year faculty; structured evaluation against transfer-student persistence, course completion, and sense-of-belonging measures (external evaluator); and a published implementation playbook so pathway pairs can replicate without us.

## Outcomes and measures (30 months)

Six pathway pairs in production (≥1,500 students, majority from low-income or first-generation backgrounds); transfer-student course outcomes and persistence vs. matched comparison **[pre-register design with evaluator]**; instructor adoption and retention at the two-year side; cost-per-student of the subsidized tier, published, as the basis of a sustainable fee model.

## Why us, why now

The founder is a transfer student who now leads training for an NSF cyberinfrastructure program; the founding team includes nonprofit governance and program-development experience; the platform exists and is in production, not proposed. The GHC sunset gives a 12–18 month window in which every computing department must choose new infrastructure — the cheapest moment there will ever be to make the equitable choice the default.

## Budget sketch

Two-year-side instructor support & course release stipends ~$160K; hosted-tier subsidy for pathway colleges ~$90K; curriculum co-design (open license) ~$70K; external evaluation ~$80K; program lead (part-time) ~$110K; convening/travel/dissemination ~$40K.
