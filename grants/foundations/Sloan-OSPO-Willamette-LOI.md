## The opening

Every existing Sloan-funded OSPO retrofits open-source practice onto a century-old research university. Willamette's School of Computing and Information Sciences — with an MS in Data Science launching August 2026 — offers the inverse experiment: build the OSPO *into* a computing school's founding years, where curriculum, student work, and institutional infrastructure can be open by default rather than by exception. A small liberal-arts university also tests whether the OSPO model works below R1 scale — the question most of American higher education needs answered.

## What the OSPO does

(1) **Policy and stewardship:** institutional open-source policy, licensing guidance, and contribution pathways for faculty and student work — drafted once, in public, as a template for small institutions. (2) **An anchor project in production:** EduCloud — the open-source classroom infrastructure already running SCIS courses — gives the OSPO a living system to steward from day one, including its student-contributor pipeline (course-embedded contributions, summer maintainerships) so computing students graduate having *maintained* real infrastructure, not merely used it. (3) **A regional open-source commons:** shared self-hosting, training (Carpentries-model workshops), and stewardship services extended to Chemeketa Community College and regional partners — the OSPO as infrastructure for institutions that could never staff their own. (4) **Sustainability beyond the grant:** by month 24, the OSPO's operating costs fold into SCIS's base budget plus modest service revenue, with the playbook for sub-R1 OSPOs published openly.
