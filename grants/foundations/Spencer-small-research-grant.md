**Working title:** Does tool continuity across the two-year/four-year boundary affect transfer students' persistence and belonging in computing?

**Request:** $49,800 over 18 months

**Why this exists alongside the NSF IUSE proposal:** this is the deliberately small, fast, fundable core of the IUSE research agenda — one sharp question, answerable on pilot infrastructure that already exists. If IUSE funds, this study nests inside it (and Spencer permits that complementarity to be disclosed); if IUSE doesn't, the research program survives anyway.

## Central research question

When community-college computing courses use the same professional version-control workflow as the receiving four-year institution — so that the *tools*, and the student's own portfolio of work, travel across the transfer boundary — do transfer students show (a) smaller post-transfer drops in course performance and persistence, and (b) stronger workflow self-efficacy and computing belonging, relative to transfer students from conventionally tooled sections?

## Significance

Transfer-student attrition in computing is well documented; interventions target advising, articulation, and finances, but the *micro-environment of coursework itself* — the tooling discontinuity every transfer student silently absorbs — is nearly unstudied. The construct (continuity of practice as a belonging signal) speaks to communities-of-practice theory; the setting (an open platform that runs identically at both institution types, now propagating through the GitHub Classroom migration) makes it a naturally occurring, scalable lever if the effect is real.

## Methods

Quasi-experimental, two pathway sites ([Pima→UArizona] / [Chemeketa→Willamette]). Cohorts: transfer students from EduCloud-tooled two-year sections vs. matched transfer students from conventional sections (propensity matching on prior GPA, credits, demographics; n≈120–160 across two cohorts). Outcomes: first-two-term computing course grades, persistence to the next course, validated belonging and self-efficacy scales (pre/post), plus semi-structured interviews (n≈24) on the transfer experience of workflow. Analysis: mixed-effects models with site fixed effects; qualitative thematic coding integrated through a convergent mixed-methods design. IRB at both institutions; pre-registration of the quantitative design.

## Timeline

Months 1–3 IRB, matching, instruments; 4–12 cohort 1 data; 8–15 interviews; 13–16 cohort 2 entry data; 15–18 analysis and writing.
