**Working title:** Open infrastructure for open materials: a community-governed platform and an openly governed AI tutor grounded in OER

**Request:** $300,000 over 24 months

## Why this sits in Hewlett's lane

Open educational resources solved the *materials* problem; the *infrastructure* those materials run on is re-closing. Computing and data-science courses — among the most OER-rich fields — depend on assignment platforms, autograders, and now AI tutors that are proprietary, surveillant, or both. When GitHub Classroom retires in August 2026, the default replacements are commercial. An openly licensed textbook delivered through closed, extractive infrastructure is only half open.

EduCloud is the other half: an open-source, self-hostable course platform whose pilot curricula (a mastery-based data-literacy/Python transfer bridge) are **Creative Commons licensed by policy**, whose **Registry** is an open catalog and metadata standard that makes openly licensed courses, assignments, and datasets discoverable and remixable across institutions, and whose architecture stores no student records at all.

The forward-looking piece is **Sol**, an optional, off-by-default AI tutor: pedagogically constrained (guides rather than answers — the guardrailed-tutor pattern recent classroom research supports), **grounded in the openly licensed course materials themselves**, transparently governed (open prompts, open evaluation, a published refusal list — no proctoring, no engagement metrics, no student profiling), and runnable on institution-controlled open-weight models. Hewlett's own grantees are converging here — BCcampus's current grant includes an AI tutor for openly licensed materials — and the field needs a reference implementation whose governance is as open as its license.

## What Hewlett funding does

(1) Bring the Registry's OER dimension to v1.0 — open metadata standard, license-aware search, one-click remix of CC-licensed course templates — with the standard developed in public with the OER community. (2) Develop and openly evaluate Sol against OER course materials at pilot institutions, publishing the prompts, evaluation harness, and learning-outcome results regardless of direction. (3) Publish the governance pattern — how a community institution makes AI-on-OER decisions accountable — as a reusable artifact for the open-education field.

## Outcomes (24 months)

Registry metadata standard v1.0 with OER fields, adopted by ≥3 institutions; ≥20 CC-licensed course templates in the public catalog; Sol evaluated in ≥4 courses with published open results; a governance playbook for open AI-on-OER. **[Insert Fall 2026 pilot data and the CC-licensed bridge curriculum as evidence.]**
