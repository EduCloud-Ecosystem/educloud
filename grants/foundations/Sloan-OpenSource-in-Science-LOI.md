**Project title:** EduCloud: a governed, sustainable open-source successor to GitHub Classroom — and a working model for how academic software infrastructure survives its corporate sponsor

## The problem

On August 28, 2026, GitHub retires GitHub Classroom, the tool through which thousands of computing and data-science courses distribute, collect, and autograde version-controlled assignments. Tens of thousands of instructors must migrate; GitHub is steering them toward commercial replacements. This is the pattern Sloan's Open Source in Science program names: research and teaching infrastructure built on a corporate platform, withdrawn when priorities shift, with no community institution standing behind it. The sunset is also a rare natural experiment — an entire academic software ecosystem forced to re-platform at once — and the window to establish an open, community-governed alternative closes within roughly two migration cycles (Spring and Fall 2027).

EduCloud is that alternative, already substantially built: an open-source (AGPL-3.0 core, Apache-2.0 interoperability primitives), self-hostable assignment and autograding platform with pluggable Git-host adapters (GitHub and Forgejo complete, GitLab next), privacy by architecture (it stores Git usernames, never student records), sandboxed container-based grading, and a one-operator deployment path. A 1.0 ships this summer; multi-institution pilots run in Fall 2026.

## What we ask Sloan to fund — institutions and incentives, not feature development

Consistent with the program's focus, this request funds none of the core feature work (which proceeds on project labor and is separately proposed to NSF). It funds the parts that determine whether the software *survives*:

1. **The managing organization.** Stand up the governance charter, fiscal-sponsored entity → 501(c)(3) transition, contributor policy (DCO), and an Ostrom-informed decision structure designed to outlive the founder. The founding team already includes nonprofit board-governance/legal experience (Ben Markwart, Cooper Center for Environmental Learning) and nonprofit program/grant development experience (Angela Cruze).
2. **The EduCloud Registry as an interoperability primitive.** An open metadata standard and lightweight, self-hostable catalog through which courses, assignments, datasets, and computational artifacts become discoverable and reusable across institutions — federating mature open systems (Coolify, Keycloak, JupyterHub) behind thin adapters rather than rebuilding them. The standard, not the platform, is the contribution to the field.
3. **An economic model others can copy.** Design and publish the sustainability mechanics — philanthropy-subsidized hosted onboarding for under-resourced institutions transitioning to fee-supported service (the model CUNY's Transfer Explorer validated in 2026), institutional memberships on the Carpentries pattern, and paid migration services — with open books, so the next orphaned academic tool has a playbook.
4. **A student-contributor pipeline.** Structured pathways (with partner institutions including a community-college transfer pilot) through which students become maintainers — the RSE workforce question Sloan has repeatedly funded, applied to teaching infrastructure.

## Why this team

The founder leads training/education for an NSF cyberinfrastructure program (MESA) and directs instructional computing at the University of Arizona; the platform reuses NSF-funded compute (ACCESS/Jetstream2) rather than buying its own. Five faculty adopters across institution types (R1, liberal arts, regional public, community-college pipeline) are committed to Fall 2026 pilots. NSF IUSE and PESOSE proposals (January/March 2027) cover the education research and ecosystem scaling respectively; Sloan support is the bridge that ensures a governed organization exists to receive that scaling.

## Outcomes by month 24

A 501(c)(3) (or sponsored equivalent) with published governance and open finances; the Registry metadata standard at v1.0 with at least two independent implementations; ≥25 institutions in production with ≥5 on subsidized hosting; ≥10 non-founder code contributors under DCO; and a published, reusable sustainability playbook for academic open-source infrastructure.

Personnel (part-time executive/technical lead, part-time RSE, governance/program lead) ~$300K; community and contributor program ~$60K; legal/incorporation/audit ~$35K; convenings and travel ~$30K; fiscal-sponsor/administrative ~$25K. Compute is in-kind via NSF ACCESS.
