### 1. Funding that arrives with me — and flows through Willamette

Both proposals are drafted, both are designed so **Willamette is the submitting institution** once I'm here — external research dollars, F&A recovery, and a visible research line, immediately additive at a school this size.

### 2. The flagship: EduCloud — and why the window is now

I'm building **EduCloud** *(working name; repo "Quad")*, an open-source successor to **GitHub Classroom**, which shuts down **August 28, 2026** — new classroom creation has already ended, and every institution that relied on it must migrate this year. EduCloud is host-agnostic, **privacy-by-architecture** (it stores Git usernames, never student records), self-hostable, with sandboxed autograding, an optional guardrailed AI-tutor layer, and a registry/federation layer in design that catalogs and publishes courses, projects, and student work on infrastructure institutions control.

- Willamette becomes the **academic home** of a platform with a national user base — visibility and a recruiting signal a small school cannot manufacture organically.
- **No IP or liability burden:** copyright sits with the independent project (fiscally hosted by a 501(c)(3) open-source sponsor, with a founding team that includes nonprofit-governance and legal experience). Willamette administers grant-funded *activities*, never the codebase.

### 5. A living teaching asset

I'm proposing a **structured hybrid**, not "fully remote": a defined in-person cadence each term (intensive blocks, advising weeks), hybrid delivery suited to the MSDS's working-professional format, and explicit availability and service commitments in writing.
